A public entry point for TMO validation artifacts, TMO-LE evidence semantics, provenance-aware inference, epistemic assessment, traceability, diagnostics, and PTM answer-accuracy evaluation. Current architecture TMO-LE evidence validation TMO-LE separates preserved resources, assertions, evidence, provenance, derivations, qualified dimensions, epistemic assessment, conflict resolution, calibrated evidence scoring, thresholds, escalation, and audit history. Preserved resource → provenance → evidence qualification → qualified dimensions → proposition evidence score → epistemic assessment → threshold / escalation → audit Foundation TMO-LE foundational invariants Interpretation may reduce uncertainty only when additional evidence justifies that reduction. Transformation alone cannot create knowledge. LE-101 Preservation before interpretation Preserve what was observed before deciding what the observation means. LE-102 Assertion is not fact Recording a proposition does not establish that proposition as fact. LE-103 Unknown is not zero or pass Missing or unresolved information remains explicit rather than becoming a default value. LE-104 Independence is origin-based Multiple records derived from one origin do not become independent corroboration. LE-105 Finding strength is provenance-bounded A finding cannot become stronger than the evidentiary provenance supporting it. LE-106 Temporal precision cannot be manufactured Derived temporal claims cannot become more precise without additional evidence. LE-107 Relations remain separate Association, attribution, coordination, causation, and authorization require separate evidentiary support. Identity and role Resolution model Identity and role are evaluated independently. Resolved identity does not automatically resolve role, agency, attribution, causation, or authorization. Resolution states RESOLVED PROBABLE MATCH POSSIBLE MATCH UNRESOLVED CONFLICT Epistemic Layer Evidentiary status without truth inflation Epistemic status describes what the available evidence presently supports. It does not transform an assertion into an unqualified fact. Epistemic states UNASSESSED SUPPORTED PARTIALLY SUPPORTED CONTRADICTION INCONCLUSIVE WITHDRAWN Qualified dimensions Verification applies only to the named dimension. For example, verified identity does not verify role or authorization. UNVERIFIED PARTIALLY VERIFIED VERIFIED FAILED INDETERMINATE Evidence weighting Proposition-specific evidence assessment Evidence weight belongs to the relationship between an evidence item and a proposition. Weight does not establish truth and cannot exceed provenance support. Relevance How directly the evidence bears on the proposition. Context Whether the evidence applies within the proposition's relevant circumstances. Specific source identity Whether the particular source is sufficiently identified for the use. Authenticity Whether the item is adequately supported as what it purports to be. Independence Whether the evidence represents an origin independent of other evidence being counted.
